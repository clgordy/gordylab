Bench Research

Experimental biology across microbial ecology, fungal genomics, and genetics. These projects connect questions about organisms and their environments with hands-on laboratory work, genomic tools, and undergraduate research.

Scientific Research Areas

Current work in the Gordy-Goller research environment spans host-associated microbial communities, fungal diversity and genome variation, and the biology of bacterial model systems.

Wax Worm Microbiomes & Genomics

Studying wax worms and their associated microbes to investigate host-microbe interactions, adaptation, and biological features relevant to plastic biodegradation.

Fungal Genomics

Exploring fungal diversity and genome-level variation to understand the mechanisms that shape how fungi survive, interact, and evolve.

Delftia Research

Connecting microbial ecology, genetics, and functional traits in Delftia species through laboratory observations and genomic data.

Wax Worms, Microbes & Plastic Biodegradation

The greater wax moth larva, Galleria mellonella, is a tractable system for studying the biology of plastic decomposition and host-associated microbes.

From field samples to genomes

Research described in the CV and recent presentations examines plastic decomposition and genetic features in wax worms. Samples collected from North Carolina apiaries, alongside commercial controls, provide material for comparing natural populations. Work with high-school researchers developed methods for DNA extraction, library preparation, and nanopore sequencing, producing a genome for annotation.

Host-associated communities

Current projects bring together wax-worm microbiomes and genomic tools to investigate how microbial communities and host biology relate to adaptation and plastic degradation. The work offers opportunities to connect bench observations with bioinformatics and genome analysis.

Related undergraduate research includes analysis of plastic decomposition and genetic features in Galleria mellonella (NC State research symposium, 2023).

Fungal Diversity & Yeast Genetics

Fungal genomics and genetics link organismal diversity to questions about cellular processes, adaptation, and useful biological traits.

Yeasts from Sugar-Seeking Insects

Research has examined diverse bread-making yeasts isolated from sugar-seeking insects and the attributes that may make these strains useful in baking. The work involved 672 listed undergraduate contributors and connects microbial diversity with observable biological traits.

Madden et al. 2021, Yeast.

Emerging Model Yeasts

Current work includes development of genetic tools for Kluyveromyces lactis and other emerging model yeasts, expanding the organisms available for genetics and cell-biology research.

Cell-Cycle Genetics

A developing research direction adapts classical genetic screening approaches to K. lactis to identify cell-cycle mutants. This work connects fundamental yeast genetics with questions about the conservation and diversity of cell-cycle regulation.

Microbial Ecology & Functional Traits

Genomic information becomes most useful when it can be interpreted alongside observations of organisms, their interactions, and their environments.

Delftia Species

The Gordy-Goller research hub describes ongoing Delftia work through questions in microbial ecology, genetics, and functional traits. Undergraduate projects have included growth-curve analysis, providing a way to pair laboratory phenotypes with genomic and bioinformatic questions.

Research Across Methods

Across projects, bench experiments, sequencing, genome annotation, and computational analysis provide complementary ways to investigate biological systems. Undergraduate researchers contribute to work from experimental design and sample processing through data analysis and presentation.

Selected Scientific Research

The broader scientific foundation includes work in yeast signaling, immunology, and cellular biology, alongside current microbial and fungal research.

Yeast Signaling & Cell Biology

Prior work includes systematic analysis of F-box proteins in the yeast mating pathway, research on autophagy and apoptosis, and student projects examining autophagy-related processes and wild yeast isolates.

Rangarajan et al. 2019, Journal of Biological Chemistry; Gordy & He 2012, Protein & Cell.

Immunology

Earlier research investigated immune-cell survival and homeostasis, inflammasome activation, CD8+ T-cell memory, and mechanisms of target-cell killing.

Gordy et al. 2011, Blood; Gordy et al. 2014, PLoS ONE.
